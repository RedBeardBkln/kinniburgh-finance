# Task Request: credit-card-full-pay (forecast enhancement)

## Owner rule (stated 2026-10-09, verbatim)
"All credit cards should be factored in to pay the entire statement balance. The minimum payment never applies as all credit cards are paid off in full each month, regardless of the amount. Please factor this into the formulations."

## What is already true (verify in code, do not re-derive)
- `lib/forecast.ts` `generateCardStatementPayment` already projects the FULL `ccStatementBalance` on `ccDueDate`; `lib/cc-funding.ts` (`analyzeCardFunding`) uses full statement balances; `CardDue.minimumPayment` is carried but unused in any calculation; `lib/notifications.ts` (`checkCardPaymentsDue`, `checkCcFundingShortfall`) passes `ccMinimumPayment` through (verify whether any text or threshold uses it).
- The Upcoming ledger (`lib/upcoming-ledger.ts`) includes the card statement on file, and a "Past due date, may already be paid" box for a statement whose due date passed (live example: Barclay $623.19 due Oct 5, 14-day window).
- `ccMinimumPayment` is still surfaced to the owner/AI: `lib/advisor/tools/list-accounts.ts` returns `minimumPayment` and its description mentions it; `app/accounts/page.tsx` / `components/accounts/accounts-page-client.tsx` carry the field (check whether it is rendered). Plaid sync stores it (`lib/plaid-sync.ts`).

## The real gaps to close
1. **Future statements are not projected.** Only the statement currently on file is in the cash-flow forecast, the Credit Card Funding analysis (30 days) and the Upcoming ledger. Because every card is paid in full, the NEXT statement(s) within the 30/60/90-day windows must be projected: for each credit card, estimate the next statement balance from real data (account `currentBalance` owed vs the statement already on file, charges posted since the last statement close, and a defensible expectation of charges until the next close/due date from recent card activity), place the payment on the next due date (same day-of-month rule; use the clamped month-length helper), label it `estimate` with a plain "why", and feed it to: (a) the Personal cash-flow forecast / funding-account analysis (the funding account must hold these), (b) the Upcoming ledger (new item kind or reuse card statement with `estimate` confidence; counted in totals? Planner to decide with justification, default: counted but clearly marked estimate, because the owner pays in full), (c) the 14-day schedule if inside it. Never double-count: card SPENDING must not also appear as bills/learned recurring items paid by the card, and the card PAYMENT must not also appear as a transfer or recurring bill (see the card-payment descriptor exclusion in `lib/recurring-detect.ts`). Business cards (EK Consulting Capital One) are funded from the entity's own checking: determine from live data which account pays each card (look at actual payment transactions) and keep entity separation (ground rule 6); if the funding account cannot be determined, say so and do not guess.
2. **Past-due statement ("may already be paid").** With the full-pay rule, detect whether the statement was actually paid: look for a payment transaction to that card (credit-card 'payment received' inflow on the card account and/or matching outflow on the funding account near/after the due date for about the statement amount). If found, mark it paid and drop it from the past-due box; if not found, keep the existing wording. Never claim paid without evidence.
3. **Minimum payment must not appear anywhere it could mislead**: remove `minimumPayment` from the advisor `list_accounts` output and description (tests pin tool shapes: update them), remove or hide it in any Accounts-page rendering if shown, and ensure no notification/ledger/forecast text or threshold uses it. Keep the DB column and Plaid sync as-is (additive-only schema; no migration). Add a small guard test that the forecast/ledger/cc-funding/notification modules never read `ccMinimumPayment`/`minimumPayment` for any computation.
4. **Wording**: observational ("expected statement payment ~$X, estimated from this cycle's charges"), never a guarantee; state the estimate's basis and confidence.
