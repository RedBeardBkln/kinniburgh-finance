## Answers to the questions asked

1. **Ground rule 1 (no fabricated numbers).** Holds. A gate is `{status, kind, reason}` and nothing else (pinned by a test, and I read every gate path in `electricEstimate`, `oilEstimate`, `firewoodResult`). Every estimate carries `basis` and `confidence`; an unseen month is the season mean, never 0; the Electric result is LOW with 8 payments in 8 months and says so ("Low confidence: not every calendar month has been seen yet") and shows the leave-one-out figure that supports it ($145 vs the flat $172's $196, n = 8, one winter: "direction only" is how it reads). Oil is always labelled "Low confidence", spread evenly, and says it does not know gallons. The oil price is only what the owner types. The two constants in code (solar since 2023-03, furnace service 29,246 cents) are owner statements, dated `statedOn`, kept in one `ENERGY_SITE_FACTS` object.
2. **Ground rule 6 (entity separation).** Holds; I traced `selectSitePayments`. Own-entity rows are attributed per site; another entity's rows are read only when (a) the row is a McCarthy-heating payee, (b) the site has an `oil` line, and (c) the other entity's slug is in that SITE's `oilFromEntitySlugs`. `sudden-valley` has `[]` and an unknown slug gets no facts at all, so no path lets another entity's rows into Sudden Valley. `setMcCarthyNotOil` repeats the same boundary (an SV transaction cannot be marked on the Personal house, an EK row cannot be marked on SV). The EK-card inclusion is labelled in the card ("paid on the ... card by mistake; read here, nothing changed") and in the basis and carries the bookkeeping/tax-time note.
3. **Ground rule 5 / auth.** `requireAuth()` is the first statement of all three exports (and pinned by a source-reading test); zod validates ids; AuditLog carries entity id, entry/transaction id and list size only (no price, date, note); the note is refused if it looks like a personal number; only account nicknames are read. The loader has no auth by design and is called only from pages / actions / cron that already authenticate.
4. **Ground rule 8.** Wording is observational ("based on your payments", "looks like", "unconfirmed"). The one soft spot is the heading "Suggestion:" on the furnace-service line; it only offers to record an annual item, acceptable.
5. **Owner statements.** McCarthy oil heats the house (EK-card rows read in, labelled). The $292.46 is the yearly furnace service: excluded from the oil figure, shown separately, annual item offered. The $1,036.75 Oct 9 repair: not auto-excluded and not hard-coded; it appears under "Check these McCarthy charges" (untagged) and the owner marks it with one click; no transaction id exists in code or seeds. Solar since 2023-03 with net dollars as the primary quantity: implemented and stated in the basis. Sudden Valley is a separate site with the rental-since-2026-04 caveat in its basis and gated with honest reasons (5 months of Eversource; no oil on its own books).
6. **D1: variable lines now carry their flat budget forward. Recommendation: keep it ON.** The owner's rule is "carry forward as budgeted; electric / oil / firewood are handled by the model", and the request text (Part 1) says their budgeted figure "remains the fallback until the model has enough data". Today the model has passed its gate for exactly one of the five variable lines (Personal Electric), so with flat carry off, Personal Oil / Firewood and Sudden Valley Electricity / Oil would have NO budget figure in every 2027 month: the business forecast, the Pace card, the assistant and the two budget alerts would silently read that as zero, which understates, and that is worse than showing the owner's own budget. The loader opt-in is one line (`carryVariable: true`), the resolver default stays false and is pinned, and the model is still consulted first by every consumer that has a plan. Condition: S3 and S4 below (label carried figures), because D1 widens exactly the places where a carried figure has no caption.
7. **Oil gate (every counted payment on or after the first price entry) vs the plan.** Agreed with the Coder's tightening. The plan said payments before the first price are "excluded and counted"; excluding them silently would understate the year, and the Coder's gate names the exact date to enter, so it fails loudly instead. Cost: the owner must enter one price dated on or before the oldest counted payment (Dec 2, 2025 today) and one at least 6 months later; the form text and the gate reason say so. Nit N2 asks for one more sentence in the basis.
8. **Hand draws win, no double count.** Verified in `seasonalEvents`: with draws, `generateBillOccurrences` produces the draw events untouched and the model starts at the FIRST of the month after the last draw's month, so no month can hold both; `replaceDraws` is the only way the model overrides, default off, no UI. Past draws are ignored by the engine as before. With a gated model there is no plan and the old path runs byte for byte. The ledger path adds `requireTailDay` so a missing pay day yields no tail event rather than a guessed date. Tests cover each case (integration file, "no month holds both").
9. **Fail-soft vs throwing.** The change is correct and is what the step 1 review asked for (a transient read error must not silently swap the owner's variable-line set for the default). Consumers: the Budget loaders that already rejected on a Budget read error (ledger input, Forecast page, advisor, `checkBudgetOverspend` / `checkBudgetPace`) now also reject on an AppSetting read error, which is the same database and the same failure domain, so no new failure mode in practice; the date index and the seasonal loader wrap the read fail-soft (`failed: true`, flat figures); the recurring hint still returns null. The cron behaviour is unchanged from step 1 (a rejected `Promise.all` skips dispatch rather than sending a wrong alert). The pre-existing mocks that lacked `appSetting.findUnique` were updated; I read the diffs of two of them, they add a mock only. No regression found.
10. **Estimate propagation to notifications: acceptable with one correction.** Budget alerts and bill reminders compare to the owner's budget / bill amount and do not use the estimate: acceptable and consistent with "an estimate is not a commitment". But it is NOT fully absent: `lib/notifications.ts` `notifyFundingAccount` calls `loadScheduledFlows`, which now passes the plans, so a LOW-confidence estimate can change the card-funding shortfall notification. That contradicts the repo's stated rule for card estimates ("only high / medium ones can send a notification"). Dormant today (the Electric bill is on "Heating & Electric"; it only bites if a card-paying account carries a seasonal bill), so should-fix not blocking (S2).

## Findings

### Blocking

### Should-fix
- **S1 `actions/seasonal-settings.ts` `setMcCarthyNotOil` + `lib/seasonal-energy-build.ts` (a mark is lost when a pending row posts).** The mark is stored by transaction id. `lib/plaid-sync.ts` archives a pending row and adds the posted row under a NEW id (it never reads `pending_transaction_id`; I counted 605 archived pending rows in the live data). The Oct 8 McCarthy rows and the Oct 9 $1,036.75 furnace repair, the very row the owner is asked to mark, are pending today. When they post the mark is orphaned: the repair returns to the counted set and to "Check these McCarthy charges", and any estimate built from it changes. Not silent (the row reappears and the basis says N unconfirmed), and the oil estimate is gated today, so no wrong number is shown now; but an owner decision is quietly undone. Minimum fix: select `pending` in the loader, carry it to the check list, and show "Pending: the bank may replace this row when it posts, and you would then need to mark it again" beside the button (or hide the button until it posts). Better: also treat a non-pending row as marked when a marked ARCHIVED row has the same account, amount and a date within a few days (needs the loader to read the marked ids' rows). Until one of those is done, tell the owner to mark the repair AFTER it posts.
- **S2 `lib/account-scheduled-flows.ts` (low-confidence estimate reaches a notification).** `readScheduledFlows` passes every plan. Its only caller is the card-funding shortfall notification, whose documented rule is that a rough (low) estimate never triggers a notification. Pass only plans with `confidence !== "low"` there (today that means none, i.e. the flat amounts, which is what the cron used before), keep passing all plans on the Forecast page and envelope page, and add one test (a low-confidence plan leaves `loadScheduledFlows` amounts at the flat figure; a medium one re-amounts). Update the CLAUDE.md consumer list sentence accordingly.
- **S3 (rule 1, labelling) `app/forecast/page.tsx` business-bucket view.** Carried figures, now including Sudden Valley Oil and Electricity, feed the business expense totals with no caption. Add the muted line planned in the step 1 review (for example "Budget figures for months without their own budget lines are carried forward from the latest earlier month"), shown when any row used has `carriedFrom`.
- **S4 `lib/notifications.ts` budget alerts.** A Budget alert on a carried row does not say so; with D1 a winter "Electric over budget" alert against a carried flat $172 will fire while the seasonal page says to expect about $573. Add the optional suffix ("budget carried forward from <period>"); also consider a short clause that the seasonal estimate for the month is higher. Follow-up acceptable.

### Nits
- N1 Performance: `loadSeasonalEnergy` (a 37-month, nine-predicate ILIKE transaction scan plus about six other queries) now runs separately for the Forecast page header, the Seasonal card, the 14-day / chart paths via the page, the ledger (also on the dashboard widget), `loadScheduledFlows` per funding account, the envelope page and every advisor budget / forecast call. Wrap `loadSeasonalEnergySafe` in React `cache()` keyed on the day, or load once per request and pass it down. Fine at 3k transactions; revisit before step 3 adds more.
- N2 `oilEstimate` basis: add "prices between your entries are assumed unchanged until the next entry" (the restatement uses the step-function price in force), so two entries a year apart are not read as a measured trend.
- N3 The furnace-service rule is an exact-cents match (29,246). Next year's service at a slightly different price would be counted as oil and, being within the 25%-300% band, would not appear under "Check these". Either widen the check list to flag a row within 20% of the known service amount, or note that the per-row toggle is the remedy.
- N4 `lineKindOfTag` / `PAYEE_PATTERNS` are regexes on names ("electric" matches any tag path containing it). Fine for the default and the owner's setting, but if the setting gets a UI, validate that the chosen tag maps to a kind and show the ones that do not.
- N5 Untracked `pnpm-workspace.yaml` and `scripts/setup-eva-account.ts` are not this step; make sure they are not staged with it.
